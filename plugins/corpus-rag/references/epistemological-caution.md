# The Epistemological Caution

This blockquote is the canonical caution text. Every skill shows it
verbatim beside every alpha or kappa number. The `codify reliability`
command prints the same sentence without the blockquote mark. Do not
reword it, shorten it, or summarize it: the identical wording is what
lets a reader recognize it everywhere.

> **Caution:** alpha and kappa measure how much coders agree. They do not say whether a code is true, whether the quotes are honest, or whether the codebook fits the corpus. A high number can hide a shared mistake; a low number can mean the material is rich. Treat the number as one input; the judgment stays with you.

## Why the caution exists

Agreement numbers answer a narrow question: did two coders produce the
same decisions on the same material? They say nothing about three
bigger questions:

- Is the code the right lens for the material? Two coders can share a
  wrong lens and agree perfectly.
- Is the evidence honest? A verbatim quote gate protects against
  invented quotes, but not against a quote that is real and misleading.
- Does the analysis fit the question? Agreement cannot fix a method
  that answers a different question than the one the user asked.

A reflexive study goes further and refuses the numbers entirely (see
the paradigm gate in the codify skills). A codebook study reports them
with this caution attached. Neither path treats agreement as proof.
