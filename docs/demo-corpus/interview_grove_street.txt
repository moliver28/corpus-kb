Priya: Thanks for sitting down with me today. Can you start by telling me how you usually get groceries for your household?
Dana: I take the number 12 bus, or I ask my sister when the bus cuts service. There is no full grocery store left in the neighborhood, so it is a forty minute trip each way.
Priya: What happens in winter, or when the bus is late?
Dana: Mostly I just make do with the corner store. Prices there are brutal and the produce is wilted, but the bus with two kids in a stroller is not realistic.
Priya: You mentioned prices. How do food costs fit into your monthly budget?
Dana: They eat almost a third of it. I work part time, so the week before payday we are stretching rice and beans and whatever is on sale.
Priya: If price and transport were solved tomorrow, what would you want easy to get?
Dana: Fresh vegetables, honestly. There is a community garden on 5th that lets neighbors grow beds, and I would join in a heartbeat if the evening hours worked with my shift.
Priya: Have you used any assistance programs, like SNAP or a mobile market?
Dana: I get SNAP, but half the corner stores do not take it for fresh items. The mobile market truck comes Thursdays, and that is the one day I plan meals around.
Priya: Last one: who do you turn to when food runs short before the end of the month?
Dana: My church pantry, and my neighbor and I swap bulk staples. It works, but it should not take a whole village to keep a fridge stocked.
