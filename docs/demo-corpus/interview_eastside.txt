Sam: To start, can you walk me through how food shopping usually works for you in a typical week?
Marcus: My wife and I share a car with her mother, so grocery runs happen on Saturdays only. If the car is unavailable we skip it, and that is when the freezer gets emptied.
Sam: What does a typical grocery trip look like when you do make it?
Marcus: We drive twenty minutes to the discount warehouse. Bulk rice and frozen bags mostly. Fresh things we buy small because they spoil before we can get back.
Sam: You mentioned cost drives the warehouse choice. Can you say more about the budget side?
Marcus: On a fixed disability check every dollar is planned. When utilities spike in summer, the food line shrinks first. The food pantry on Grand Avenue covers the gap maybe twice a month.
Sam: Are there foods you wish were easier to find close by?
Marcus: Fresh fish and greens. There used to be a market on 9th before it closed. Now the closest thing is a gas station.
Sam: Do programs like SNAP or the school meals make a difference for your household?
Marcus: School meals are huge. In summer they run the park program and that stretches breakfast. SNAP helps, but the warehouse club does not accept it, which is backwards.
Sam: Finally, when money is tight, who or what helps your household get through?
Marcus: Honestly the church van that brings produce boxes on Sundays. Neighbors text each other when it shows up late. It should not depend on a text chain, but it does.
